# The Casting-Out — the liturgy of denunciation in New Harnomy

> Direction from Brad, 2026-09-04: *the rhetoric of the denunciation must reflect the solemn tone of the
> Bildersatzian catechism. The proceeding echoes a religious ceremony where the sinner is cast out from the
> company of the righteous. It becomes a "teachable moment," in the way the execution of the regicide was to
> instruct the community — Foucault, Discipline and Punish.*
>
> This file is the rhetoric bible for every scene in which New Harnomy expels one of its own. It governs
> **NH-S346** (Pengold casts out Dick) and, retroactively, Dick's own campaign against Philip Winters in
> Acts I–II — which must use the same liturgy, so that when it is turned on him the audience recognises the
> form before he does.

---

## 1. The thesis: New Harnomy has one sacrament, and it is expulsion

A church knows it is a church by whom it casts out. The Congregational meeting-houses of Massachusetts
Bay did not merely tolerate excommunication; the public admonition, the confession before the whole body,
the vote of the members, and the delivery of the sinner "unto Satan" were the acts by which the
congregation *constituted itself as the saints*. Anne Hutchinson's trial in 1637 is the American original:
a righteous community, a heterodox member, a proceeding conducted in complete sincerity by people who
believed they were saving her soul, ending in expulsion. New Harmony, Indiana — the town this city is named
for — sits directly downstream of that culture.

The Framework is New Harnomy's catechism; the mauve book is its Bible; the Feast is its Mass; and **the
casting-out is its only sacrament with teeth.** Everything else in the liturgy — the land acknowledgment,
the gratitude inversion, the raised books — is preparation for the moment the community proves its purity
by producing an impurity and removing it.

Pengold does not invent this ritual for his own ends. He *uses* a ritual the community already loves.
That is what makes him effective and what makes the community complicit.

## 2. Foucault's scaffold, and ours

*Discipline and Punish* opens with Damiens the regicide, 1757, torn apart before a crowd in the Place de
Grève. Three things from that account govern this opera:

**(a) The condemned is the text, not the audience.** The lesson is not for Damiens. It is for the crowd.
Power writes itself on his body so the community can read it. In NH-S346 the denunciation is not addressed
to Dick, and Pengold never once looks at him. Dick is *read from*. Everyone else is being taught.

**(b) The crowd is required, and the crowd is dangerous.** The spectacle needs witnesses — and Foucault
notes the crowd could turn, could pity the condemned, could riot against the executioner. A casting-out that
loses the room becomes a martyrdom. Every denunciation in this opera therefore contains **one moment where
it could tip** — where the condemned says something human and true and the room almost hears it — and the
denouncer's real skill is shown in how he closes it. (Pengold closes it by absorbing the sympathy onto
himself: *"I do know that. That is the part I am going to have to live with."* The executioner suffers more
than the condemned. Robespierre's move: virtue as visible pain.)

**(c) The modern form does not destroy the body. It reforms the soul — and calls it kindness.** Foucault's
argument is that the scaffold gave way to discipline: the examination, the confession, the timetable, the
"gentle way in punishment," which presents correction as the natural, even loving, consequence of a
diagnosis. **This is the Bildersatz register exactly.** Nobody in New Harnomy is punished. They are
*held accountable*. They are *invited to step back*. They are *given space to do the work of repair*. The
vocabulary is therapeutic and the effect is total. Mao's formula for the rectification campaigns — *cure the
disease to save the patient* — is the same instrument in different upholstery.

And so the trailer and the keynote are one object. The **scaffold in the square** (NH-S901) is the 1757
form, built in timber, in public, by cheerful men. The **denunciation at the Feast** (NH-S346) is the
modern form — no rope, no body, only a diagnosis and a room full of people agreeing. The opera puts them
four days apart so the audience can watch one become the other.

## 3. The lineage, and what each contributes

These are structural borrowings. No real speech is quoted, no real person is named on stage, and the
dialect throughout is New Harnomy's own — contemporary institutional-progressive English, used sincerely.

| Source | Structure borrowed | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| **Catechism (call and response)** | Question / fixed answer, known by all. The congregation does not deliberate; it *recites*. | The Ten Principles as the chorus's responses. Pengold states the case; the chorus answers with the Principle that closes it. Dick knows every response. He may mouth them. |
| **Anathema — bell, book and candle** | A physical object removed or extinguished to mark the soul's departure from the body of the faithful. | **Dick's mauve book is taken from him.** The chorus raises theirs; his is the only one lowered, then gone. It rhymes with Valencia dropping hers in the finale — she lets go by choice; his is removed. |
| **Congregational discipline (Massachusetts Bay)** | Admonition → public confession → the *vote* of the members → delivery. The community assents on the record. | The chorus's affirmations are the vote. Pengold uses meeting-facilitation grammar — *"I'm hearing alignment"* — to take it. Consensus process as excommunication. |
| **Moscow trials, 1936–38 (Vyshinsky's prosecutions)** | The confession is the centrepiece and the accused must *agree*; treason is total and retroactive (every good act was secretly sabotage); the prosecutor does not prove guilt, he *reveals* it; the people demand the verdict. | Dick is invited to "acknowledge." He explains instead — *"that's how the work gets funded"* — and the room hears a confession. His nine years of work are reread as nine years of harm. |
| **The struggle session (批斗会)** | *Bow your head and admit guilt.* The accused is denounced **by intimates** — students, colleagues, family; the crowd chants; anyone who defends the accused becomes suspect; the accused thanks the masses for the education. | The denunciation by an intimate is **Summer's single nod.** She never speaks. Pengold invokes her — *"Summer has already taken the harder step"* — and she inclines her head once. It is the loudest thing in the number. |
| **Speaking bitterness (诉苦)** | Victims testify against the accused before the assembly. | The woman on Halsted testifies — **but she is not present.** Pengold reads her into the record. The working class is spoken *for*, which is Principle 1 enacted (*the people do not yet know what they need*). Valencia, the only working-class person on the stage, is not asked to speak. |
| **Mao, "Combat Liberalism" (1937)** | The first named liberalism: *to let things slide for the sake of peace and friendship when a person has clearly gone wrong.* Friendship as the enemy of rectitude. | *"Accountability that stops at the door of a friend is not accountability. It is decor."* |
| **Mao, "cure the disease, save the patient"** | Punishment reframed as medicine; the accused is a patient; the assembly is therapy. | The entire register. Nobody says *punish*. They say *hold, invite, step back, repair, space*. |
| **Mao, the Hunan report** | *A revolution is not a dinner party* — the refusal of hospitality as a revolutionary virtue. | The Anti-Thanksgiving Feast: a meal that announces it is not a meal. *"We do not give thanks here. We give account."* |
| **Stalin — the two-faced one (двурушник)** | The enemy within is worse than the enemy without because he *passed*; his loyalty was the disguise. | *"He knew the words. That is what makes this hard."* Dick's sincerity is reread as camouflage. Principle 7 turned against its own believer. |
| **Castro, "Words to the Intellectuals" (1961)** | *Within the Revolution, everything; against it, nothing.* Critique of method = opposition to the whole. | Principle 9 verbatim: *"Those who question the path oppose the destination."* Dick's one line is a question about method. The syllogism closes on him in real time. |
| **Chávez, the televised expropriation** | The leader names the enemy live before the assembled people, and the people respond on cue; the naming is the show. | The Feast is broadcast. The naming happens in front of the cameras New Harnomy invited. |
| **Lenin, 1918: "hang them — and I mean hang so the people can SEE"** | Visibility is the pedagogy. Punishment out of sight teaches nothing. | Why it is done at the Feast and not in an office. Why the scaffold is in the square and not behind city hall. |
| **Robespierre — the Festival of the Supreme Being** | A revolutionary liturgy: the state stages a feast-day for its own virtue, with the leader presiding as priest. | The Anti-Thanksgiving Feast *is* this. Pengold presides. (Robespierre was on the scaffold within two months; the opera does not go there, but the audience that knows will feel it.) |
| **Robespierre — "virtue without which terror is fatal"** | The denouncer's pain is the proof of his righteousness. He suffers more than the condemned. | Pengold's closing: *"I am going to be forgiven for it by everyone except…"* — he makes himself the wounded party. |
| **Contemporary populist "enemies of the people" discourse** (Cambridge EJIS; OU 2025) | Existential threat conjured; a *single named cause*; the betrayal narrative; the speaker as the only true representative of the unheard; emergency justifying exception; character destroyed in place of argument engaged. | The whole architecture of §III–§VI. Dick is the single cause. Pengold speaks *for* the woman on Halsted. The application deadline is the emergency. His argument is never answered, only his character. |

## 4. The Ten Principles as liturgy

The chorus knows the Principles the way a congregation knows the creed. In a casting-out they are used as
**responses**: the denouncer names a case; the chorus answers with the Principle that disposes of it.
Quoted verbatim always (`THE_FRAMEWORK.md` rule). The pairing below is canon for NH-S346 and for Dick's
campaign against Philip:

| Move | Principle used as the response | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Dismissing the accused's good faith | **2** *"Harm is structure, not intent"* | His sincerity is irrelevant. Nothing he meant can be entered in evidence. |
| Answering his defence of method | **9** *"Those who question the path oppose the destination"* | His explanation becomes proof of opposition. |
| Answering his appeal to conscience or friendship | **7** *"Individual conscience is false consciousness"* | His inner certainty is the colonizer speaking through him. |
| Speaking for the absent victim | **1** *"The people do not yet know what they need"* | Why the woman on Halsted need not be present. |
| Requiring the room to assent | **3** *"Silence is complicity"* | Anyone who does not raise a book has chosen. |
| When the room flinches | **6** *"Discomfort signals growth"* | The flinch is the proof it is working. |
| The close | **10** *"The collective will emerges through correct consciousness"* | The vote was never a vote. The will was already there; the assembly merely revealed it. |

Principle **3** is the trap under the whole opera. It is used here against the room to compel assent — and
it is, unheard by anyone on stage, the verdict on Summer, who is silent, and on the house, which is silent.

## 5. The seven stations of a New Harnomy casting-out

Every expulsion in the piece follows this order. A director should be able to find all seven in NH-S346
and, in shortened form, in Dick's treatment of Philip.

1. **The Welcome** — warmth, gratitude to the room, the community affirmed as good.
2. **The Inversion** — the ordinary virtue (thanks, hospitality, friendship) is named as the enemy of the
   higher one (account, rigor, the work).
3. **The Arithmetic** — numbers read flatly. Facts, all true. The room does not yet know what they are for.
4. **The Naming** — the accused's name, spoken with love. *"…is my friend."*
5. **The Litany** — the harms, one by one, each answered by the chorus with a Principle. The absent victim
   read into the record. The accused offered the chance to "acknowledge"; whatever he says is heard as
   confession.
6. **The Intimate** — someone close assents. In 346, Summer's nod. The moment the room could tip, and the
   denouncer absorbs the sympathy onto himself.
7. **The Removal** — the object taken (the book), the assent taken (the raised books, *"I'm hearing
   alignment"*), the accused delivered. The denouncer names his own pain as the last word.

## 6. Rules that survive everything

- **Every accusation is true.** A false accusation makes a frame-up and a thriller. True accusations make a
  satire about what truth is for. (NH-S346 QA rule 2.)
- **Nobody raises their voice, nobody sneers, nobody shouts a slogan.** Struggle sessions were shouted.
  This one is *supportive*. The register is the horror.
- **Nobody says "punish," "guilty," "traitor," "enemy," or "expel."** The vocabulary is entirely
  therapeutic: hold, acknowledge, harm, impact, space, step back, repair, the work, alignment.
- **Nobody on stage hears the joke.** No character comments on the form, recognises the precedent, or
  notices that the Framework is being used as a weapon. Only Valencia *sees*, and she is not yet permitted
  to speak.
- **The denouncer never looks at the accused.** He is read from, not addressed.
- **The Principles are quoted exactly.** Never paraphrased into new ones. They are scripture.
