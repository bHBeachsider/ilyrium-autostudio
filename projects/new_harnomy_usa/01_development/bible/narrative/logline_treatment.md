# Logline / Treatment — NEW HARNOMY, U.S.A.

> Source of truth: `../../research/NEW_HARNOMY_USA_Overview_v11.pdf` (Working Draft v11, 37 pp).
> This file restates v11 in the Stage-1 bible form. It adds no plot. Where v11 is silent, this file says so.

**NEW HARNOMY, U.S.A.** — *A Revolutionary Opera in Three Acts*. Feature-length satirical opera:
48 musical numbers, ~3 hours with two intermissions. Cast: 3 principals (soprano, tenor, baritone) +
Valencia (mezzo-soprano, narrator / Greek chorus of one) + ensemble.

## Logline

During Thanksgiving week in the fictional city of New Harnomy, **Summer Winters** — soprano,
executive director of the LYSTERIA Coalition — needs **$5 million in city matching funds** from
**Mayor Pengold Heavens** (baritone) to unlock a $50 million federal grant, but overhears that Pengold
will buy federal cover by sacrificing her lover, the trust-fund organizer **Dick Suede** (tenor), at
the Anti-Thanksgiving Feast; she says nothing, signs the paperwork, and watches him led away in
chains — at which point the only person who can name what happened, the working-class organizer
**Valencia Strong** (mezzo), asks the house the question the whole opera has been asking:
*"What won't you do?"* Tone: sincere, reverent, no winks — the comedy is entirely in the gap between
the characters' sincerity and the absurdity of their positions.

## Premise

A three-hour satirical opera that **inverts the structure of Maoist Cultural Revolution model
operas** to examine how contemporary progressive movements avoid class analysis while focusing on
identity, culture and discourse. Satire *and* tragedy: absurdist elements, real emotional stakes.
Three main characters represent aspects of contemporary activist culture; one narrator stands outside
it. The city's name is the thesis: **New Harnomy** — harm embedded in harmony, a misspelling no
character ever notices — echoing Robert Owen's failed 1820s utopia at New Harmony, Indiana
("plenty of thinkers but not enough experienced farmers and craftsmen").

## Theme

**Authenticity vs. Performance** — "You Cannot Perform Your Way to Liberation." Revolutionary
rhetoric masks bourgeois emotional chaos and class privilege; the "revolution" is a personal brand,
activism a career path, resistance professionalized. Sub-themes (v11 §VIII): **The Question**
("What won't you do for the cause?"), **The Monopoly on Truth** (Pengold's paternalism), **The Absent
Economic** (principals never discuss money, class, labor or ownership; only Valencia can — and is
unheard), **The Selective Blindness** (they know colonialism's crimes and are willfully blind to
liberation's), **The Cycle Continues** (nothing fundamental changes; new season, new sacrifice).

## Central dramatic question and stakes

*Will Summer speak?* The engine is a modest sum — $5M matching funds gating a $50M federal grant —
chosen deliberately: "she's not Faust selling her soul for infinite power — she's a middle manager
protecting a line item." The stakes are a man's freedom, then his life; the opera refuses to resolve
whether Summer will ever speak.

## Structural laws (must hold in every stage)

1. **The Wildean Principle** — characters remain completely earnest and oblivious to the ironies
   around them. No winks, no self-awareness. (Kernel register: "sincere reverent serious register,
   no irony, no comedic timing, no winking.")
2. **The Economic Silence** — Summer, Pengold and Dick never discuss economics or material
   conditions. Only Valencia does, and she is invisible/inaudible to the principals.
3. **The Selective Lens** — the principals are well-credentialed, theory-fluent, and interpret
   everything through colonial domination / systemic oppression / structural violence; blind to
   leftist authoritarianism.
4. **The mauve books** — copies of *A Framework for Progressive Awakening* ("the Framework",
   "Bildersatz, E.E.") are visible in every location; all mauve. See `../THE_FRAMEWORK.md`.
5. **Three load-bearing silences** — Pengold's 22 seconds of reading; Dick's clarinet line
   ("Pengold — you know me"); the eight-second blackout. Never shortened. See `../PRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md`.

## Structure — three acts, Thanksgiving week

| Act | Title | Days | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | "The Gathering" | Mon–Wed before Thanksgiving | Pengold alone reading headlines about federal investigators, already calculating his sacrifice. The city-sponsored "New Harnomy People's Thanksgiving Festival" begins (land acknowledgments, workshops, fundraising galas). The triangle forms. Dick, naive and hopeful, awaits his promised GRIFT grant. Summer's affair with Dick continues while she negotiates matching funds with Pengold. Dick's "donation" shakedown escalates across the act (charming → insistent → threatening). |
| II | "The Crisis" | Thu–Fri | Tensions escalate. Summer overhears Pengold's plot to sacrifice Dick (The Betrayal) and stays silent to protect her funding. The funding scandal begins to break. Dick remains oblivious, still believing Pengold protects him. Somewhere here: the "asymmetry" scene (Dick remembers their first meeting; she doesn't). |
| III | "The Reckoning" | Sat — Ersatzgiving | The Feast: Pengold's keynote turns celebration to denunciation; Summer's Socialist-Realist portrait rises; Dick is arrested in chains; the Mafia-Wife Coronation (Pengold's hand on Summer's shoulder); the confirmation ("LYSTERIA's allocation is confirmed"); Valencia's final aria; news of Dick's suicide; the unresolved chord; hard cut to black; eight seconds. |

## Arc (beginning → end state)

- **Summer**: genuine believer → active collaborator in a betrayal; corrupted "through incremental
  accommodations, each one reasonable in isolation, until she found herself complicit in a man's
  death." End: frozen beneath her own glorified image, hand on shoulder, choice unmade.
- **Pengold**: the intern who watched → the boss operating the same machinery with new slogans;
  cannot separate love from strategy; end: "settled" — the smallest possible smile, held.
- **Dick**: charming performer who half-suspects none of it is real → the designated villain in
  someone else's revolutionary theater → a body. "Little Dickie finally made his mark."
- **Valencia**: joined believing PILF's original mission → slow, silent recognition that "the drift is
  not a corruption of PILF. It is PILF now." End: opens the mauve book, closes it, lets it fall,
  sings one line to the house.

## Ending (fixed)

The Six Beats of the Finale (v11 §X) are the staging blueprint against which every closing choice is
measured — see `../../../04_preproduction/storyboards/NH-S348_FINALE_SIX_BEATS.md`. Ambiguous ending:
the opera does not answer whether Summer will ever speak. No curtain call as normally understood
(see `../PRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md`). "A satire. A tragedy. A warning. A witness."
