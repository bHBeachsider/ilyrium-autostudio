# Comedy and tragedy — the governing decision on the laugh

> Brad's question, 2026-09-04: *Is this satire with the humor left out? Will the audience find any humor in
> the absurdity of the characters, their contradictions and the plot — or should humor be left out and make
> this a full-blown absurdist Greek tragedy?*
>
> This file is the CCO's answer and the rules that follow from it. It sits beside `tone.md`, which it does
> not contradict — it explains how `tone.md`'s "sincere, no winks" can be executed without producing the
> failure mode Brad is worried about.

---

## 1. The answer

**It is a tragedy in which the audience discovers the comedy.** Not a comedy played straight and not a
tragedy with the jokes removed: a piece in which every character is inside a tragedy and every spectator is
inside a satire, at the same time, in the same frame.

The two are not in competition. They are the same event seen from two seats. Pengold casting out his
friend at the Feast is, to Pengold, the hardest thing the movement has ever asked of him. To the house it is
a man reading a grocery receipt as an anathema. Both are true. The frame does not choose. **The frame holds
both, and the audience's laugh is the sound of it noticing.**

So: the humor is not left out. It is left *unperformed.* That is a different thing, and the whole craft
problem is keeping the difference visible.

## 2. Why not go all the way to tragedy

The pull toward "full-blown absurdist Greek tragedy" is real and worth respecting — the piece already has the
bones. Valencia is a Greek chorus of one. The news of Dick's death arrives inside an aria the way a messenger
speech arrives. The chorus raises books like a stasimon. The ending is a frozen tableau. Pengold sacrificing
the young man to preserve the order of the city is Creon, and Summer beneath her own portrait is very nearly
Clytemnestra. None of this needs adding; it is there.

But tragedy and satire ask the audience for different things, and the difference is the whole argument of
the piece.

**Tragedy asks for pity and fear.** It consoles. It lets the audience feel that what happened was terrible
and inevitable and larger than the people it happened to. If New Harnomy is a pure tragedy, Pengold becomes a
tragic figure — trapped, suffering, forgivable — and the house leaves pitying him.

**Satire asks for recognition and shame.** It does not console. It says: this is not fate, this is a choice,
and you have made a version of it. The opera's last line is *"What won't you do?"* — offered to the house.
That is not a tragedian's lament. It is a satirist's question, and it only lands if the audience has spent
three hours *seeing through* Pengold rather than suffering *with* him.

This is Brecht's argument, not Sophocles'. *Threepenny Opera* is an opera that is a satire that plays
serious, and its entire method is refusing the audience the comfort of pity so that they are forced to
judge. New Harnomy is in that line. Going to pure tragedy would trade the knife for the consolation, and the
piece exists to be the knife.

## 3. Why "satire with the humor left out" is a real risk — and what actually causes it

Brad's worry is the correct worry. The failure mode exists and it has a specific mechanism.

"Sincere, no winks" fails when the absurdities are **too soft to register** or **too slow to land.** The
audience senses that something should be funny, cannot locate it, is not sure it is permitted to laugh, and
the laugh dies in the throat. What is left is earnestness: a solemn piece about people the audience is
meant to find ridiculous but has not been given a clear enough view of. That is the tedium Brad is naming.

Opera is unusually prone to this, for a mechanical reason: **a joke that takes twenty seconds to sing is not
a joke.** Comedy lives in speed and in the beat after. Arias are slow by design. So a satirical opera that
puts its absurdities into arias will bury them, however sharp the writing.

The fix is therefore *not* to add humor. It is to make the contradictions harder-edged and to control where
they sit in the musical fabric. Which gives the rules.

## 4. The rules

**Rule 1 — Never perform the joke. Ever.** `tone.md` stands. No character knows they are funny. No
delivery telegraphs. No camera comments. The models are Wilde, whose characters never know; Kubrick's
*Strangelove*, where the war room is played as a war room; Iannucci's *Death of Stalin*, whose rule on set
was that everyone plays it as real terror — and it is real terror, and it is hilarious; and above all Adams,
Goodman and Sellars' *Nixon in China*, an opera played with total reverence in which the house laughs at
Nixon's banality and is moved by him in the same minute. That is the exact register.

**Rule 2 — Comedy in the recitative, tragedy in the arias.** This is Mozart and Da Ponte's architecture and
it is the structural answer to §3. The laugh lines are *fast and dry*: recitative, parlando, or spoken.
*"I'm hearing alignment."* *"It is decor."* *"That's how the work gets funded — you know that."* Each is
three seconds. The feeling — Pengold's real sorrow, Dick's plea, Valencia's question — is sung, and given
all the time it needs. The audience is never asked to laugh at something slow or to weep at something fast.

**Rule 3 — Give the laugh air.** A contradiction needs a beat of silence after it or it cannot be heard.
Motifs §6, *cut on stillness*, is the comedy's breathing room as much as the tragedy's. After *"It is
decor"* the chorus responds — and then nothing, for a count of two, before Pengold resumes. That gap is
where the house laughs, or does not, and either is allowed.

**Rule 4 — Sharpen, don't soften.** When a contradiction is not landing, the instinct will be to explain it
or to lighten it. Do neither. Make it *more* exact. The Ten Principles are funnier sung verbatim than
paraphrased. The grocery on Halsted is funnier with the number of years attached. The scaffold is funnier
built by cheerful men than by sinister ones. Precision is the joke.

**Rule 5 — The acronyms are never said as jokes.** LYSTERIA, PILF, TWEEEDS, the Man Bun Collective. They are
proper nouns to everyone on stage, said with the flat familiarity of people who have said them a thousand
times. The house hears them once, freshly. That asymmetry is the entire comic mechanism of the naming, and one
raised eyebrow from a performer destroys it.

**Rule 6 — Let the tragedy be real.** The comedy only has weight if Dick's death is not funny. It is not.
The clarinet line, the eight seconds, Valencia's book on the floor — these are played for what they are, and
the house must be permitted to be devastated. A satire that cannot wound is a sketch.

## 5. Where the laughs are, concretely, in what already exists

So that this is not theoretical — the house will laugh, if the rules above are followed, at:

- **The catechism responses in №46.** *"St. Adalbert's parish, four thousand dollars."* / *"Harm is
  structure, not intent."* The laugh is recognition: this is exactly how these rooms sound.
- *"I'm hearing alignment."* Excommunication in the voice of a facilitator. Flat, fast, spoken.
- *"Accountability that stops at the door of a friend is not accountability. It is decor."* Funny and
  terrible in one breath, which is the target register for the whole piece.
- **Dick's one honest line** — *"that's how the work gets funded"* — being the line that damns him.
- **The scaffold**, built in the sunshine by men who are pleased with their work.
- **Pengold in Tammany Hall**, not knowing whose room it is (`world/factions.md`: he does not know who Boss
  Tweed was).
- The whole architecture of a *Feast* that announces it is not a meal.

And the house will *not* laugh — must not be asked to — at Dick turning at the threshold to look at Valencia,
at the hand on the shoulder, at the thump of the book, or at the eight seconds.

## 6. How we find out

This is the one question in the bible that can be tested rather than decided. **The pilot exists to answer
it.** When the trailer and the two bookends are cut, show them to people who do not know the piece and say
nothing. Watch where they laugh, where they almost laugh, and where they look away. If the laughs are absent,
the fix is Rules 2–4, not the addition of jokes. If the laughs are in the wrong place — at Dick's exit, at the
chord — the fix is Rule 6 and a colder performance, not a warmer one.

The failure Brad fears is real, and it is a failure of *execution*, not of *conception*. The conception is
right. This file is how the execution stays honest to it.
