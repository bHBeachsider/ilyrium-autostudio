## Deep background (real history the opera mirrors)
- **1825** — Robert Owen buys Harmony, Indiana from the Rappites.
- **1826** — the "Boatload of Knowledge" arrives (scientists, educators, artists).
- **1827** — the communal experiment effectively collapses (lack of skilled labor; internal friction;
  financial strain on Owen's fortune). Town survives as an intellectual colony; legacy → Smithsonian,
  public education, USGS HQ, feminist/abolitionist hub. Today a National Historic Landmark.
- **1996** — the single authorized portrait of Bildersatz (kernel casting canon: "1990s continental
  theorist; single authorized 1996 portrait").

## Backstory (relative to the opera's present)
- **~10+ years ago** — Summer begins running LYSTERIA.
- **~5–6 years ago** — Summer marries Philip Winters (classics professor; Peloponnesian War / Athens).
  Marriage lasts five years.
- **4 years ago (election night)** — Pengold's first city-council campaign victory party. Summer ran the
  campaign as consultant/organizer ("work spouse" dynamic; her spouse resents it). Dick, young and new to
  activism, meets Summer briefly, starstruck; she barely remembers him. That same night, later: Summer and
  Pengold's drunken encounter — "the unspoken thing between them ever since." Pengold's fixation begins.
- **~3.5 years ago** — Summer and Philip separate. The marriage lasted five years.
- **~3 years ago** — Summer undergoes irreversible sterilization without consulting Philip. **After the
  separation**, Philip publishes his article questioning NGO effectiveness (Owen's New Harmony; Sparta vs
  Athens; "the warm embrace closes like a fist"). PILF (Dick's organization, by now more established)
  organizes the protest campaign; the university pressures Philip to resign; he is effectively driven out.
  Summer watches from a distance, says nothing. **She is still legally married to him.**
- **~2.25 years ago** — Summer runs into Dick at a coalition meeting; he reminds her of election night;
  "Bildersatzian sparring as foreplay"; he mentions the campaign against "that professor," proud of it;
  she realizes *"Wait, what professor?"* — understands exactly what he did, and says nothing about the
  connection. Coffee → drinks → dinner. **The affair begins while the divorce is still pending.**
- **2 years ago** — Summer's divorce from Philip is finalized. The affair is already a few months old.
  *(CANON DECISION 2026-09-01 — reading C. See "Divorce and affair" below.)*
- **~6 months ago** — Summer launches The Man Bun Collective under LYSTERIA, built for the $50M federal
  "Gender-Affirming Reproductive Healthcare" grant. Pengold has been "preparing this leverage for months."
- **Recently (weeks)** — Dick discovers PILF's shortfall ("money is missing and I don't know where it went")
  while preparing the GRIFT grant application; cannot report it. Federal investigators begin circling
  New Harnomy's NGO ecosystem. Pengold learns of the affair "almost immediately" (a coalition meeting, a
  TWEEEDS operative) — and does nothing; waits.
- **Now** — the affair is **just over two years old** — "long enough to be serious, recent enough to still
  be passionate, old enough that they've settled into patterns" (v11). Summer's federal application is due
  right after Thanksgiving; the Festival is the Man Bun Collective's official launch.

## Divorce and affair — CANON DECISION (2026-09-01, Brad)

v11 is internally inconsistent by a year: p.6 says the divorce was "finalized one year ago"; the Act II
timeline passage says "two years ago... (one year ago from the opera's present)". Three readings were put
to the writer. **Reading C is canon:**

> **The divorce was finalized two years before the opera's present, and the affair with Dick began before
> it was final.**

What this fixes:
- The affair gets its full run. "Settled into patterns" now has time to have happened.
- Both v11 passages are accounted for: the "two years" is the finalization; the "one year" reading is
  discarded as a drafting artifact.

What this costs (one bible edit, deliberate):
- Summer's bio line "recently divorced (finalized one year ago)" becomes **"divorced two years ago."**
- v11's "the affair is 1–2 years old" becomes **"just over two years."**

Why it is the strongest reading dramatically — **it makes her silence active, not passive.** Under the
one-year reading, Summer is a widow of her own marriage who later takes up with the man who destroyed her
husband's career. Under reading C she was *already with him*, still legally married to Philip, when she
learned what Dick had done — and stayed. The "Wait, what professor?" beat survives intact and gets worse:
she finds out, and chooses him anyway.

This reframes three things downstream and every one of them should be played:
1. **"She may have even quietly encouraged it."** No longer speculation the audience weighs. She was in
   the bed of the man running the campaign while it ran.
2. **"What won't you do for the cause?"** Philip's question was asked by a man who was, at the time, still
   her husband — and who may have known. It stops being abstract.
3. **Summer's rationalization cascade in Act II** gains a floor beneath it. She has done this before,
   at smaller scale, and told herself the same things. Dick's sacrifice is not her first silence; it is
   her second. That is what makes the ending cyclical rather than tragic.

**Staging note:** nothing in the libretto should explain this. It sits under the performance the way the
mauve books sit on the shelves. The audience should be able to reconstruct it and never be told.
