# Setting

> Source: v11 §VI (Setting and Timeframe), §I (Historical Foundation).

**NEW HARNOMY | the whole world of the piece | mid-century-municipal + professional-class-progressive interiors, tungsten, winter | mauve books everywhere**

## Place

The fictional American city of **New Harnomy** — "a factionalized American city where ideological purity
tests determine social standing and performative activism has replaced actual governance." The name
deliberately echoes **New Harmony, Indiana** (Robert Owen's failed 1820s utopia). The misspelling embeds
*harm* in *harmony*; no character ever acknowledges the spelling — to them it is simply the town name,
pronounced "New Harmony." The audience sees what the characters cannot.

## Period

Contemporary (present day). Thanksgiving week: Monday through Saturday (Anti-Thanksgiving Day).
Winter light, short days, cold. Deadline pressure: Summer's federal grant application is due right after
the holiday; charitable-giving season = peak NGO performance.

## The event that drives everything

**The New Harnomy People's Thanksgiving Festival** — a week-long, city-sponsored "alternative
Thanksgiving" leading to an **Anti-Thanksgiving Day** culmination (the Feast). **Official name of the day**
(Brad, 2026-09-05): **Anti-Thanksgiving Day of Atonement** — the city's own wording, on the banner and in the
program. The Festival's stated aim is to decolonize Thanksgiving and replace its mythology with one that atones
for the sins visited on Indigenous peoples by European settlers. The word is exact and no one on stage hears it:
the Feast is an atonement rite in the oldest sense — a community proving its purity by loading its sins onto
one body and driving it out (`THE_CASTING_OUT.md`) — and it atones for four centuries by putting Dick Suede in
chains. The name must sound written by the administration's comms office, never by Indigenous organizers; real
Indigenous-led observances (National Day of Mourning, Unthanksgiving Day) stay out of the opera entirely.
Officially sponsored by
Mayor Pengold's TWEEEDS administration, co-organized by PILF (Dick) and The Man Bun Collective (Summer),
funded by city grants — the patronage machine in action. Marketed as "Decolonizing gratitude, centering
marginalized voices, reimagining abundance." Land-acknowledgment ceremonies, workshops, fundraising galas,
display tables of mauve Framework copies. "The workers they claim to represent are serving them at their
events."

## Historical mirror (why the name matters)

Robert Owen bought Harmony, Indiana from the Rappites in 1825; the 1826 "Boatload of Knowledge" brought
scientists, educators and artists; by 1827 the communal experiment collapsed on three contradictions —
lack of skilled labor, internal friction over governance/"equality", and financial strain (Owen's fortune
funded it — the very class privilege the commune rejected). The town survived as an intellectual colony
(Smithsonian, public education, USGS HQ, early feminist/abolitionist hub). Owen's failure is Valencia's
critique of the fictional city; the "Boatload of Knowledge" is the precedent for the principals'
credentialed inability to discuss economics. Philip Winters's article uses this parallel explicitly.

## What the setting is NOT

Not a cartoon, not a dystopia, not a stylized "satire set". It is a real, plausible mid-sized American
city photographed as prestige political drama. The absurdity is entirely in the people.
