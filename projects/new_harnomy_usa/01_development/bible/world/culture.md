# Culture / economy

> Customs, hierarchy, economy that shape behavior. Source: v11 §I–§V, §VIII.

## Hierarchy (who outranks whom, socially)
Framework fluency is the currency of status. Credentials (elite prep schools, Ivy League, graduate
programs) admit you; competitive Bildersatzian display ranks you (PILF staff underline passages "in
competitive colors... trying to demonstrate superior consciousness"). Ideological purity tests determine
social standing. Above the fluent sits the funder: Pengold's machine, which allocates grants and thereby
existence.

## Economy (the thing no principal will name)
- **Patronage, not programs.** Public money → aligned NGOs → dependency → political power. "The problem IS
  the solution": problems must be managed, never solved, because solving them would end next year's grant.
- **Everyone's books are problematic.** Whim-allocated grants, creative reporting, manufactured outcomes.
- **Class origins are the invisible engine.** Summer: old money, socially costless radicalism. Dick: trust
  fund funding a "rebellion" against family wealth. Pengold: clawed up from a TWEEEDS summer internship
  by watching how power actually works. Valencia: the only one whose economic constraint is real and
  immediate — "the rent is due."
- **The shakedown as fundraising.** PILF's "voluntary contributions" solicited from a family bodega, an
  independent restaurant (immigrant-owned "gentrification enablers"), a Black church and a parish
  ("regressive institutions"), and an academic who questioned NGO effectiveness (the existential target).
  Targets are "considerably smaller and more vulnerable than the rhetoric suggests."

## Customs and rituals
- **Land acknowledgments, workshops, fundraising galas** (festival week).
- **The Framework as liturgy**: quoted in grant applications, board presentations, media interviews;
  displayed like scripture; raised like hymnals; the program book printed in mauve.
- **"Please silence your phones in solidarity with our most vulnerable"** (house manager).
- **Performative discretion**: everyone knows everyone's business ("New Harnomy's progressive community is
  small"), and pretends not to.
- **The polyamory proposition is theoretically sophisticated, not an affair** (Summer's framing).
- **Cancellation**: cancelled friends briefly mourned then forgotten; trials mean being called out and
  feeling bad.

## Speech and rhetoric (feeds VOICE & SPEECH blocks)
- Principals speak in Framework vocabulary: biopower, structural violence, praxis, consciousness, false
  consciousness, decolonize, centering, the warm embrace, collective care, accountability.
- Dick's core frame: *warm embrace of collectivism vs. coldness of rugged individualism* ("individualism
  dies and collective flourishing begins"; "Once they stop resisting... they'll feel the relief of not
  having to stand alone").
- Summer's frame: Principle 2 (harm is structure) and Principle 5 (process is praxis) as defenses; "we're
  destabilizing the normative assumptions that render certain bodies invisible."
- Pengold's frame: Principle 9 (those who question the path...) and Principle 4 (the framework reveals
  truth) as immunization against criticism; "the people" always "they".
- Valencia's idiom: direct, economically precise; translates rhetoric into material reality ("He loves them
  so much he'll never let them speak for themselves... the people don't need saving — they need him to get
  out of the way").
- Philip's (offstage) idiom: Thucydidean; Sparta vs Athens; "the warm embrace closes like a fist";
  "what won't you do for the cause?"

once this week; ushers in Veja sneakers with independent-bookstore totes; natural wine at the bar.
