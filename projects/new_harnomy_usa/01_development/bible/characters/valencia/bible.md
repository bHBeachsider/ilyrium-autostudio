## ✅ REVISED — CANON 2026-09-02 (Brad)

Valencia is being re-conceived. The lines above are the ORIGINAL kernel/v11 reading and are retained until
the casting slate settles the face. **Where this block and the lines above disagree, this block is the
intent.**

**Age: LATE TWENTIES (decided 2026-09-02), not late forties.** She is the youngest person in the room and the only one who can
see. She was taken into the TWEEEDS circle as a novitiate — initiated, not raised in it — and from inside
has watched the farcical absurdity of the platforms and the people. She goes along to get along because she
needs the job. She has none of the family resources the others fall back on.

**Wardrobe: hipster styling worn as a corporate uniform.** She *appears* hipster; she is not *of* hipster
the way Dick and Summer are. Theirs is a consciously performed revolutionary bona fide. Hers is what you
wear to this office to not be noticed. Visually the two rhyme and the money does not: Dick's look is curated
at expense, hers is assembled from what was affordable. Same silhouette family, opposite bank balances —
which is the Economic Silence made wearable.

### Ethnicity — DELIBERATELY AMBIGUOUS (canon 2026-09-02, Brad)

She is **not identifiably of any single region.** Warm mid-tone skin, dark eyes, dark hair; features that do
not resolve to one part of the world. Name no origin in any prompt, ever.

This is structural, not decorative. **Her POV is the universal truth.** Every other principal reads the world
through identity categories and deploys them as credential — it is the mechanism by which the Framework
converts a person into a position. Valencia cannot be sorted, so the apparatus has no purchase on her. Cast
her to a region and the only clear sight in the opera becomes one more subject position, dismissible by the
same move the characters use on everyone else.

The kernel's `casting_canon.valencia` previously read "Mediterranean rim or European East." The first casting
slate drifted off it and the drift turned out to be the right instinct; the kernel now carries the ambiguity
as the requirement. See `canon_decision.valencia_ethnicity`.

**Practical note for prompts:** describe the *non-resolution* rather than listing regions — "features that do
not resolve to any one part of the world" — so the model blends instead of picking.

### The inversion — the load-bearing note

She has genuinely rough working hands. **She wants a manicure.** Calloused hands and fingers burnt over hot
stoves are not badges of pride to her; they are real problems she would fix tomorrow if she could afford to.
She would like to be beautiful and cannot fund it.

This inverts the trap the whole character used to sit in. The old note read "don't prettify her," which
quietly asked her to look authentically poor — the same aestheticised poverty the opera is attacking. The
correct version is sharper: **she wants to be prettified and cannot be.** Dick spends four hundred dollars on
sneakers to look like a worker. Valencia has the hands of one and wants them fixed. That gap is the thesis of
the opera worn on a body, and it is why she is the one who can see.

**Casting consequence:** do not cast her already beautiful — the wanting evaporates and she becomes ordinary
aspiration. Do not cast her as picturesque hardship either. The gap between what she is and what she wants to
be IS the character. Render the aspiration in what she has *done with limited means*: drugstore makeup applied
with care, cheap dye growing out, one small good earring, polish chipped and grown out.

**Her rough working hands remain canon for SHOTS** — they are the physical fact the manicure note turns on.
They are deliberately kept OUT of casting stills: naming a body part makes an image model feature it, and in
the first slate "rough worked hands kept in frame" produced hands raised to camera in five of six frames
(FINDINGS NH-USA-005). Cast the face; stage the hands later.

### The age question — SETTLED

Decided **late twenties**, and the kernel `casting_canon.valencia` has been updated to match (it read
"late 40s"). The synthesis was taken over the two extremes for a reason worth keeping:

- At forty-seven her idiom — v11 §VII's "knowledge that predates and will outlast the ideological fashions
  the other characters inhabit," the blues and work-song references — reads as **authority**, but "she needs
  the job" loses its bite and the novitiate framing makes no sense.
- At twenty-two everything about the job and the initiation lands, but the final aria risks reading as
  **naivety rather than clarity** — the opposite of her function, since she carries the opera's moral weight.
- **Late twenties keeps both.** Old enough that the calluses are earned and she can hold the house in the
  final aria; young enough that she is plainly the newest person in the room, that the rent is genuinely due,
  and that the manicure she cannot afford is an aspiration rather than a resignation.

FINDINGS NH-USA-004 closed. Kernel bumped with a `canon_decision.valencia_age` block.

## 🔒 LOCKED VISUAL DESCRIPTOR — CAST 2026-09-02 (VAL-H01)

**The face is chosen.** Reference: `03_design/characters/casting/valencia_v3/VAL-H01.png`, a Nano Banana Pro
blend of VAL-C05 (gravity, brow architecture, strong nose) with VAL-C06 (tiredness, softness, no armour).
Screen-tested at finale Beat 5: `casting/valencia_screentest/VAL-BEAT5.png`.

Reuse verbatim in every prompt. The LoRA reinforces this text, it does not replace it (FINDINGS on Flux
trigger binding).

`a woman in her late twenties of unplaceable ethnicity, features that do not resolve to any one part of the world, heavy dark brows and a strong nose framing steady dark eyes, the jaw unset and the mouth soft rather than held, faint shadows of tiredness beneath the eyes, dark hair worn loose and practical and slightly undone, slight build, grave but not defended, an open face that could break, secondhand layering worn as a work uniform, a thrifted cardigan over a plain tee, small cheap jewellery`

**Why this face and not the other two.** VAL-C05 alone was the authority pick but her jaw was set, which
reads as armour, and an armoured face cannot break open in the closing aria. VAL-C06 alone had the ache but
not the gravity to hold a house for ninety seconds. The blend keeps C05's structure and C06's unguardedness:
she can be still for a long time and still go somewhere.

**What NOT to do to her.** Do not make her prettier. The manicure gap is the character, and a beautiful
Valencia has no gap. "Soften" here means unsetting the jaw and opening the face, never adding glamour.

## PERSONALITY / PSYCHOLOGY
- want: to steer PILF back to its original economic-justice mission from the inside ("if she can get the
  right people in the right room, redirect the funding, remind everyone what the work was supposed to be").
- need: to keep the job — "every time she thinks about walking out or speaking up, the rent is due." The
  only character whose economic constraint is real and immediate.
- flaw: silence she cannot afford to break; "the clarity to be honest and cannot afford to" (vs. Dick and
  Summer who "have the freedom to be honest and will not use it").
- core contradiction: hands clean (no shakedowns, no endorsement of targeting) — "but she is inside the
  machine, and the machine runs on her silence."
- temperament: quiet, exact, unillusioned; not an idealist in the abstract; not cynical either — she
  saw both the trust-fund revolutionary AND the wounded human being.
- the awakening: "not a single revelation but a slow, uncomfortable accumulation" — a number here, a meeting
  there, a conversation that does not add up.
- compact with the audience: "I know this is absurd. You know this is absurd. I am going to show you exactly
  how absurd it is, and exactly why I am still standing in it, and you are going to understand both things at once."

## VOICE & SPEECH
- fach: MEZZO-SOPRANO. Her idiom stands apart: "more direct, economically precise"; may reference older
  traditions — blues, work songs, the musical language of actual labor — while remaining contemporary; her
  material "predates and will outlast the ideological fashions the other characters inhabit."
- the cellist's fragment: a short phrase in her modal idiom, seeded pre-show, returns only in her final aria.
- signature: the interlude after "They, the People" ("He loves them so much he'll never let them speak for
  themselves... the people don't need saving — they need him to get out of the way"); the final aria — what
  remains when ideologies fade: "the work that must still be done, the bills that must still be paid, the
  bodies that must be buried, the questions that must be answered"; the last line, quiet, offered to the house:
  *"What won't you do?"* — "Not accusatory. Quiet. A question."
