## PSYCHOLOGY (as it bears on Summer's arc)
- Wanted a conventional life: children, stability, intellectual partnership. Valued measured scholarship
  over performative activism; asked uncomfortable questions about funding sources and actual impact.
- During the marriage he probed gently: *"But who decides what the collective wants? What happens to the
  person who disagrees with the collective's judgment? How is what you're describing different from
  enforced conformity?"* Summer dismissed this as too abstract, too academic, "liberal hand-wringing that
  protects privilege."
- The unforgivable sin: after separation he published an article examining how professionalized activism
  becomes self-perpetuating and disconnected from community needs. Core parallels: **Owen's New Harmony**
  (thinkers without craftsmen) and **Sparta vs Athens** ("Contemporary progressive organizations increasingly
  deploy collectivist rhetoric while operating as personal fiefdoms. The 'warm embrace' of collective identity
  functions as a mechanism of control rather than genuine mutual aid... The warm embrace closes like a fist").
  Conclusion: effective organizing requires material focus, economic analysis, tolerance for dissent.
  "The academic's critique wasn't wrong — and Summer knew it. That's why it was unforgivable."
- The question that haunts the opera is his: *"What won't you do for the cause?"*
