## 📝 CASTING REVIEW — 2026-09-02

**He must be likeable, which is not the same as handsome.** The audience has to understand why Summer fell
for him and why a bodega owner opened the door. If the casting produces a face the audience distrusts on
sight, the extortion sequence becomes obvious instead of horrifying and the arrest becomes satisfying instead
of tragic. He is the only principal whose face **gives everything away** — which is exactly why he never sees
it coming.

Consequent changes:
- "lean charismatic man" — charisma is a judgement, not a feature; a model cannot render it. Now leads with
  **an open unguarded face with no wariness anywhere in it.**
- The wardrobe list was long enough to dominate the generation. Shortened — and wardrobe is a separate lock
  anyway, since the LoRA must learn his face and not his jacket.

**The Valencia rhyme — load-bearing after her 2026-09-02 revision.** Her hipster styling is a work uniform
assembled from what was affordable. His is the same silhouette curated at ten times the price to look that
way. Put them in a two-shot and the audience should see near-identical outfits and opposite bank balances.
That is the Economic Silence made wearable, and it is now written into both prompts.

Carries the caste block — see `../THE_CASTE.md`.

### The man-bun is a rhyme the characters cannot hear

Summer's new initiative is **The Man Bun Collective**. Dick's hair being man-bun-capable — dirty blond, long
enough to gather in a second — puts the organisation's name on the body of the man she is sleeping with while
she negotiates its funding. Nobody in New Harnomy ever remarks on it. The audience sees it; the characters
cannot. That is the Wildean law working exactly as v11 designs it, and it costs one wardrobe decision.

It also sits inside v11's own description of his hair: *"grown out and product-free (but regularly trimmed at
an expensive barber), the carefully maintained 'I don't care' look that requires professional maintenance to
appear effortlessly casual."*

**Staging use:** he should wear it down for most of Act I and gather it into the bun at the festival — the
gesture reads as getting-down-to-work and is, in fact, him wearing her organisation on his head.

### The softness moved from the body to the eyes

"Little Dickie" — the family called him soft — is now a fact about his **character, not his physique**. He is
gym-built and immaculate with nothing behind the eyes: no resilience, never had to be hard. A physically
impressive man with nothing underneath is more unsettling than a visibly weak one, and his collapse lands
harder for it.

**Do not make him punchable.** A face the audience wants to see arrested breaks the ending.

## PERSONALITY / PSYCHOLOGY
- want: the GRIFT grant (to save PILF and cover the missing money); Summer's approval; to be someone other
  than Little Dickie.
- need: to know "Is ANY of it real?"
- flaw: naïveté about treachery ("believes loyalty flows both directions"); a fragile ego shored up by
  serial seduction and Bildersatzian performance; lacks the force of will to rein PILF in.
- core contradiction: rebellion against family wealth funded entirely by family wealth; champions collective
  submission from a trust fund; "building Sparta while calling it liberation"; "he IS the collective he's
  demanding submission to."
- temperament: charming, earnest, passionate, present ("Unlike Pengold — calculating, patient, strategic —
  Dick is present"); the person who says "I don't condone it, but I understand it."
- arc across Act I: charming/persuasive → insistent, heavier rhetoric → explicitly threatening ("social
  media exposure, protest targeting, 'we'll remember who stood with us'") — Valencia sees the exact moment
  he crosses from organizer to extortionist.
- rationalization cascade: "I need to cover this temporarily until the GRIFT grant comes through" / "these
  businesses benefit from gentrification/patriarchy anyway" / "just redistributing resources the system stole
  first" / "I'm not stealing — I'm organizing community contribution."
- emotional default & range: warmth, conviction → in the finale, "genuine pathos despite everything";
  he does not scream.
