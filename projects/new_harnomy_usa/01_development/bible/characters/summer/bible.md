## 📝 CASTING REVIEW — 2026-09-02

**She is Valencia's mirror, and casting her should say so.** Valencia wants something she cannot afford.
**Summer has bought something she cannot return.** She runs a reproductive-justice organization and had
herself sterilized without telling her husband; the Act III portrait then glorifies her as "a mother of the
movement despite her barrenness."

None of that renders directly. What renders is that **her stillness costs her something.** A merely serene
face has not understood the part — and a face that is merely cold cannot be idealized in the portrait, while
one that is merely warm cannot freeze beneath it.

Consequent changes to the casting slate:
- The old descriptor said "composed" three different ways and never said what was underneath. Trimmed.
- "professional-class activist tailoring" is unrenderable — now described: a well-cut dark blazer over a fine
  plain knit, expensive unpatterned fabric, no logo.
- **Bare ring finger, no mark.** Two years divorced and the skin has forgotten — the same erasure she
  performs on Philip. A small renderable decision with real freight; reverse it if the writer prefers.
- The variant axis changed from hair colour to **how much the composure is holding**, which is the only
  variable about her face that is dramatically interesting.

### Her beauty is plot machinery, not decoration

v11 §III states it directly: *"She's beautiful (he's shallow, she's genuinely attractive)."* Two men are
undone by her and the drama does not run without it. **Pengold waits four years** — through her marriage, her
separation, her divorce — building leverage and calling it patience. **Dick genuinely falls**, in spite of
himself and a long roster of conquests. Cast her merely credentialed and both of those read as implausible;
the triangle deflates and the $5 million negotiation loses its charge.

*Casting note, recorded because I got this wrong:* SUM-K02 was initially marked down as "too glamorous,
reads fashion model rather than executive director." That was over-indexing on the credential and ignoring a
stated fact of the source. Brad overruled it. The correct reading is that she is **both** — beautiful and
formidable — which is precisely what makes her dangerous in a room.

**The tension to hold:** she must be beautiful AND able to freeze. The Act III portrait glorifies her; her
real face beneath it is recognition and horror. Beauty is not warmth — hers is cool, kept, and costs her
something to maintain.

## PERSONALITY / PSYCHOLOGY
- want: the $5M matching funds → the $50M federal grant → the Man Bun Collective viable, LYSTERIA
  competitive; to remain "in the game".
- need: to know whether she is capable of authentic connection or only loves the work.
- flaw: strategic ambiguity she can no longer locate the edges of; plausible deniability as a way of life
  ("Like Kay Corleone asking Michael if it kills people, Summer chooses not to know what she already knows").
- core contradiction: radicalism that is socially costless (family wealth insulates her); the promise of
  abundance (Summer) turned to barrenness (Winters) — sterilization without consulting her husband.
- temperament: composed, organized, early, professional; warm where it costs nothing.
- fears/defenses: Philip's cold questions ("But who decides what the collective wants?"); the question she
  cannot answer — "What won't you do for the cause?"; defends with Principles 2 and 5.
- status behavior: navigates elite spaces natively; a revolutionary elder to Dick; a dependent to Pengold.
- emotional default & range: composure → (rare) recognition. Rationalization cascade under pressure:
  "Dick's organization IS poorly managed", "if I warn him, I compromise myself", "Dick has family money —
  he'll be fine", "this is what revolution costs."

## BACKSTORY (load-bearing only)
Ran LYSTERIA 10+ years; launched the Man Bun Collective 6 months ago for the federal grant; **divorced two
years ago — the affair with Dick began before it was final** (canon decision 2026-09-01, reading C; see
`../world/TIMELINE.md`); complicit in the destruction of two men who loved her; overhears the plot; signs
the paperwork.

She learned that Dick had led the campaign against Philip *while she was already with him and still legally
married to Philip*, and stayed. Her silence at the Feast is her second silence, not her first — which is
what makes the ending cyclical rather than tragic. Never stated aloud; the audience reconstructs it.
