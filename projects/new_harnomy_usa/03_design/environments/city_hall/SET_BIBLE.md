# New Harnomy City Hall — set bible
### The building, the square, the Mayor's office, and the one geometry that ties them together

> Brad's draft sheets, 2026-09-04 (`reference/brad_draft_sheet_*.webp`; the CHOSEN facade is the one with
> the large semicircular stone veranda). Direction: *"turn these into sets; the interior office has the
> colonnaded veranda; align for interior/exterior continuity."*

---

## 1. The building

**New Harnomy City Hall** — American Beaux-Arts municipal, c. 1908–1914, pale grey limestone. A long flat
facade of two tall storeys over a rusticated base, with the defining feature at its centre: **a projecting
semicircular portico — a giant-order colonnade two storeys high, twelve columns on a curve — carrying a
shallow entablature, with a drum and cupola above.** Behind the columns at the upper level runs a
**curved stone veranda** with a balustrade: the "semicircular stone veranda" of the drafts. Tall French
windows open onto it from the rooms behind.

This is a real building type. San Francisco, Cleveland, Des Moines, a hundred county seats built exactly
this — the City Beautiful movement's idea of what municipal power should look like. It is *not* European,
and it must never read as a European capital: **no Haussmann rooflines, no Palazzo, no Manhattan skyline
behind it.** Beyond the square the city is low — four- and five-storey commercial blocks, a church steeple,
bare winter trees. A mid-sized American city, as `setting.md` demands.

## 2. The reconciliation with canon (read this before anything else)

`world/setting.md`, quoting v11: Pengold's office is *"Not grand — modest mid-century municipal, the kind of
mahogany-laminate authority that aspires to rooms Robert Moses actually occupied."*

Brad's drafts are grand: chandeliers, gilt portraits, a fourteen-foot ceiling. The exterior is grander still.

**These are reconciled, not chosen between, and the reconciliation sharpens the satire.** The *building* is
grand because it was built in 1910 by a city that believed in itself. The *office* is what a 1962 renovation
did to one of its rooms: the great proportions survive — the ceiling height, the tall French windows onto the
veranda, the plaster cornice, maybe the chandelier nobody ever took down — but the room has been furnished by
the municipal purchasing office. Mahogany-laminate desk and credenza. A vinyl-and-chrome visitor's chair.
Fluorescent panel light that is switched OFF in every scene, because Pengold prefers his lamp. A dropped
acoustic ceiling was proposed in 1962 and, mercifully, never installed.

So: **a room that aspires to what it once was**, occupied by a man who aspires to the men who once sat in it.
"Aspires to rooms Robert Moses actually occupied" — literally. The grandeur is inherited; the occupant is a
middle manager. v11 is satisfied, and Brad's veranda stays.

The set is offered in **two dressings** so Brad can choose the degree:

| Dressing | What survives from 1910 | What 1962 did |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Inherited grandeur** (closest to Brad's drafts) | ceiling, cornice, French windows, chandelier, parquet, one oil portrait of a forgotten mayor | the desk, the credenza, the visitor's chair, a steel filing cabinet in the corner |
| **B — Canon-forward** (closest to v11) | ceiling, cornice, French windows | everything else: laminate, vinyl, a municipal clock, the chandelier long gone and its ceiling hook still there |

Both carry the identical set dressing from `pengold_office_dawn.md`: the tungsten desk lamp still on; the
half-empty coffee; the folded newspaper; **three mauve books stacked on the credenza; one pristine mauve copy
squared on the desk**; and Summer's portrait, small, glimpsed once.

## 4. The geometry — this is the continuity contract

Everything below is fixed so that the exterior plate, the interior plate, the window POV, and the trailer's
shots SH005/SH006 agree.

- **The Mayor's office is on the upper floor, directly behind the semicircular colonnade**, centred on the
  building's axis. Its three tall French windows open onto the curved veranda.
- **From inside, looking out:** the nearest columns are *huge and close* — a metre or two beyond the glass,
  cropping the view into tall vertical slots. Then the balustrade. Then, below and beyond, the square.
- **The square** is paved in grey granite setts, roughly 60 m deep, ringed by low buildings and bare trees.
- **The scaffold / dais** stands **on the building's axis, about 40 m out**, oriented so its *back* is toward
  City Hall and its steps face the square — the dais uses the portico as its backdrop for the Feast. From the
  office Pengold therefore looks **down onto the back and top of the platform, framed between two columns.**
  He sees it as a stage-manager sees a stage: from behind, from above, with the audience's side empty.
- **Light.** The facade faces south-west, so the trailer's dusk shots put the sun behind and to the left of
  the building from the square, and the office windows get the last raking light through the columns.
- **Scale check:** a column is ~11 m tall. The dais platform is ~1.6 m high, ~9 m wide. A man on the veranda is
  ~1/6 of a column. Keep these ratios in every plate.

## 6. Continuity verified across the five plates (2026-09-04)

- The **same building** appears in the dusk and first-light exteriors: twelve-column semicircular portico,
  drum and cupola, steeple to the right, brick commercial blocks either side. Re-lighting from one plate to
  the other rather than regenerating is what guaranteed it — **always derive time-of-day variants from a
  locked plate, never from the prompt alone.**
- Both office dressings show the **giant columns a metre beyond the glass**, cropping the view into vertical
  slots, with the square far below — which is the interior half of the contract in §4.
- The POV plate shows the dais **centred between two columns, seen from above and behind**, small against
  the square — the scale in §4 holds (platform ≈ 1/7 of a column).
- Flags: American only. Books: present on the desk and credenza in both dressings. Skyline: none — a
  steeple and low rooflines.

## 7. Decision: Office A, with Brad's revision (2026-09-04)

Brad chose **A** and supplied his own revision: `plate_office_A_v2_brad_revision.jpg`. Against v1 it adds
heavy dark damask curtains with tiebacks, full dark-wood wall panelling, a polished real-wood desk in place of
laminate, and lights the chandelier's candle bulbs. The curved room, the giant columns a metre beyond the
glass and the balustrade all survive — **the continuity contract in §4 holds.**

The revision moves the room toward 1910 and away from v11's "not grand" — **deliberately.** Brad's reading,
2026-09-04: *the ornate office echoes Tammany Hall. Pengold is a grifter like Boss Tweed posing as a social
justice warrior; he wants his cake and to eat it too. The heavy drapes are more ominous than bare windows.*

This was always latent in the piece — his party is TWEEEDS, and `world/factions.md` records the triple pun
and the fact that **Pengold does not know who Boss Tweed was.** The office is now the set-design expression
of that pun: he sits in Tammany Hall without knowing it, the way New Harnomy lives in *harm* without hearing
it. The audience reads the room; he only occupies it. So the §2 reconciliation is superseded: the room is
not a 1962 renovation of grandeur but grandeur *kept* — velvet, panelling, tassels, a real desk — with the
filing cabinet and the cantilever chairs as the only municipal tells. The drapes do double work: they are
the Tammany curtain, and at dawn they make the room a box with one lamp lit in it.

Canon consequence for the character bible: Pengold's "aspires to rooms Robert Moses actually occupied" now
reads as *achieved*, not aspirational — which is worse. He is not reaching for the machine. He is in it.
