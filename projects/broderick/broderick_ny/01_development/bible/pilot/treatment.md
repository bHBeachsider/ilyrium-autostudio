# Pilot Treatment — New York, New York

**Logline:** A deadpan urban legend about a placid homeless man who haunts Penn Station and detonates supernaturally loud nonsense bellows inches from unsuspecting commuters — a city institution the NYPD has given up evicting.

**Treatment:** A gap-toothed mascot in a crooked ball cap — pizza slice in one hand, giant thumbs-up from the other — welcomes us between the Statue of Liberty and the Empire State Building: NEW YORK, NEW YORK... it's a helluva town! Then the documentary voice takes over. A few years ago, a wild-haired homeless man seemed to live in Penn Station. He didn't bother anybody. He would just walk around — and sometimes stop near someone and stand there for a while. Behind a prim businessman at an ATM, the stillness stretches... and then detonates: MMMAAOUUUB!! MMMAAOUUUB!! — a bellow of almost supernatural volume, audible to jackhammer crews at street level, leaving the old man convulsing and chest-clutching. The man drifts on, blank and serene, and repeats the blast beside a bowl-cut commuter whose phone goes airborne. A concerned silhouette reports a distressed man to the NYPD information booth, where a jaded, protruding-toothed officer shrugs: they keep throwing him out, but he keeps coming back — got a hard-on for this place. The mascot returns for the button, thumbs-up held high as a ghostly flag, bald eagle, and colonial fife-and-drum trio swell behind him. It's a helluva town. Grayscale, grotesque, and played perfectly flat.
