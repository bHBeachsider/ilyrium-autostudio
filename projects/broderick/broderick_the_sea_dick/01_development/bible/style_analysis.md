# Style Analysis — The Sea Dick, Part One (SEED v0, 2026-06-11)

Panels examined: 01 (boat establishing), 04 (creature reveal), 10 (lecture Q&A), 11 (finale). 11 panels total in `panels/`, landscape ~3:2.

## Medium determination
**Hand-drawn digital, grayscale, with a deliberate fidelity split.** Evidence: squiggle-stroke wave lines and a child-simple ray-burst sun (panel 01) are unmistakably freehand; the boat hull is a flat solid-black silhouette with white line detailing; audience members are pure black cutout silhouettes. Against this crude base, the Sea Dick's face (panel 04) carries far denser caricature crosshatching and facial modeling — a referenced/traced-feeling head on a loose cartoon body, which the strip itself lampshades in dialogue ("as if his face was traced off a photo"). Lettering is a uniform typeset bold all-caps comic font; pointer lines, not balloons.

## Line / color / shading / lettering
- **Line:** loose single-weight freehand for scenery; denser hatching reserved for the creature's face and the professor's head. Splash droplets as short flick strokes.
- **Color:** none. Grayscale tonal fields carry the scenes.
- **Shading:** flat fields — white sky, ~mid-gray sea, darker gray lecture wall. Crosshatch modeling only on hero faces. No gradients.
- **Lettering:** typeset bold all-caps comic font, no balloon outlines. Black text on white sky in sea scenes; WHITE text directly on the gray wall in lecture scenes; thin straight pointer lines run from each text block to its silhouetted speaker. Emphasis via larger point size ("SEA DICK!") and underline ("HIMSELF").

## Composition and framing habits
- Landscape panels split horizontally: white sky above, flat gray sea below; horizon line in the upper third.
- Sea scenes: subject centered in a ripple ring; dialogue stacked in the white sky.
- Lecture scenes: locked over-the-audience shot — black silhouette heads across the bottom, professor centered at podium, white-bordered video inset upper-right looping the double-bird clip; four dialogue columns with pointer lines.
- Crowd-as-silhouette is a load-bearing economy device: only the professor and the video get faces.

## Continuity risks
1. The creature's face fidelity must stay HIGHER than its surroundings — equalizing detail kills the visual joke.
2. Legal: in-strip dialogue jokes about a real-person resemblance, but generated art must NOT converge on any real likeness; regenerate from attributes per legal_rule.
3. Video inset must show the same freeze (double middle fingers, ripple ring) in every lecture panel.
4. Audience silhouette count/arrangement (4 heads) should hold across lecture panels.
5. White-on-gray lettering needs sufficient wall darkness or it washes out.

## Keep vs fix for regeneration
**Keep:** landscape format; grayscale flat fields; black silhouette boat and audience; squiggle waves and ray sun; balloon-less pointer-line dialogue; white text on gray indoors; video-player inset motif; creature's barnacled chest and double-bird signature pose.
**Fix:** the traced-photo feel of the creature's face — rebuild as an original caricature meeting the attribute spec; slight gray-value drift between sea panels; pointer lines occasionally ambiguous about which silhouette speaks; dialogue columns crowd each other in the densest Q&A panel.
